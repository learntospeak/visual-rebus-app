## Shared rules

- Keep the standard Clue Canvas puzzle-screen UI.
- The only new mechanic is the jigsaw/assembly inside the normal puzzle artwork frame.
- Rotation is available from the first puzzle.
- No guide or reshuffle controls in the player-facing UI.
- Answer field, underline pattern, compact keyboard, clues, submit and reveal follow the production Clue Canvas flow.
- Premium artwork only; no placeholder SVG, emoji scene, stock-style image, poster copy, logo, watermark or UI embedded in the art.
- Artwork must survive subdivision into puzzle pieces with clear landmarks, contrasting regions and useful edges.
- Before any image is accepted, test the actual crop at its intended grid size.

## Puzzle 6 — Too Many Cooks Spoil the Broth

- Type: idiom / proverb
- Suggested grid: 3 × 3
- Rotation: medium
- Difficulty target: 5/10
- Composition:
  - warm old-world kitchen
  - one large central broth pot
  - several cooks all interfering with the same pot
  - one salting, one stirring, one adding herbs, one tasting, one adding another ingredient
  - broth visibly becoming overcomplicated / messy without looking disgusting
  - clear left / centre / right visual zones
- Clue intent:
  1. Focus on how many people are working on the same pot.
  2. Everyone is trying to improve the same broth.
  3. The phrase warns that too many people interfering can ruin the result.
- Art direction:
  - cinematic storybook/editorial illustration
  - premium warm copper and amber lighting
  - rich kitchen detail, but distinct piece landmarks
  - no text

## Puzzle 7 — Don't Make a Mountain Out of a Molehill

- Type: idiom
- Suggested grid: 4 × 3
- Rotation: medium
- Difficulty target: 6/10
- Composition:
  - foreground starts with a tiny molehill
  - same mound visually exaggerates into an absurd towering mountain
  - small mole nearby for scale
  - dramatic clouds and alpine peak on the exaggerated side
  - composition should clearly communicate tiny problem becoming enormous
- Clue intent:
  1. Compare the size of the mound at each end of the scene.
  2. One very small problem has been exaggerated.
  3. Think of the saying about a molehill becoming something much bigger.
- Art direction:
  - cinematic landscape illustration
  - strong scale contrast
  - clear horizon, rock shapes and colour zones for slicing
  - no text

## Puzzle 8 — Get Your Ducks in a Row

- Type: idiom
- Suggested grid: 4 × 4
- Rotation: high
- Difficulty target: 6/10
- Composition:
  - detailed pond or farmyard scene
  - a line of ducks becoming progressively organised into one perfect row
  - some initially scattered visual context around the row
  - final row must be clear without making the answer instantly obvious before assembly
- Clue intent:
  1. Look at how the ducks are arranged.
  2. The scene is about putting things into order.
  3. The phrase uses ducks as the objects being organised.
- Art direction:
  - premium naturalistic/storybook illustration
  - varied water, foliage, feathers and reflections
  - strong individual duck silhouettes and landmarks
  - no text

## Puzzle 9 — Burn a Hole in Your Pocket

- Type: idiom
- Suggested grid: 5 × 4
- Rotation: high
- Difficulty target: 7/10
- Composition:
  - close-up clothing pocket with coins/notes
  - warm glowing heat from money inside the pocket
  - fabric visibly singed through into a hole
  - non-dangerous stylised flame/ember treatment
  - surrounding jacket, stitching, hand and background provide detailed landmarks
- Clue intent:
  1. Focus on what the money is doing to the pocket.
  2. The phrase describes money you are impatient to spend.
  3. The pocket is literally being damaged by heat.
- Art direction:
  - cinematic macro/editorial illustration
  - cloth texture, stitching, metallic currency detail
  - dramatic but readable warm/cool contrast
  - no text

## Puzzle 10 — Back to the Future

- Type: Guess the Movie
- Suggested grid: 5 × 4
- Rotation: high
- Difficulty target: 7/10
- Answer: Back to the Future
- Copyright-safe direction:
  - do not reproduce film poster composition
  - no actor likenesses
  - no copied vehicle design
  - no film logo or title lettering
- Composition:
  - original retro-futuristic sports car silhouette, clearly generic
  - road splitting visually between a 1980s-style streetscape and a futuristic city
  - prominent analogue clock / time motif
  - luminous tyre/road trail suggesting time travel
  - visual motion clearly travelling back and forward through time
- Clue intent:
  1. The vehicle is travelling between two different eras.
  2. Time and direction are both important.
  3. Think of an iconic movie title about returning to a future time.
- Art direction:
  - premium cinematic sci-fi illustration
  - original design language
  - neon highlights against dusk/night
  - strong road, sky, clock and city landmarks for piece slicing
  - no text

## Acceptance checklist

For every image:

- full-resolution master retained
- no heavy compression
- composition legible at mobile size
- individual puzzle crops remain recognisable
- no accidental empty rows/columns
- no embedded answer text
- no close semantic duplication with current catalogue
- no copied movie poster/logo/actor likeness for movie entries
- tested with the real Assembly renderer before approval
